      Calibrate a whole rig at once

      From one recording to a full sensor model.

      MVIS is the calibration library behind the OpenMVIS benchmark. It solves camera intrinsics and distortion, extrinsics, time offsets, rolling-shutter readout, board poses and (optionally) the board shape, camera–IMU transforms, IMU intrinsics and biases in a single GTSAM factor graph.

      It reads ROS1 and ROS2 bags through rosbags, so ROS does not need to be installed. It also reads EuRoC/ASL folders, image folders and timestamped images.

      # cameras only
$ mvis cam -c camera.yaml

# cameras + IMUs, jointly
$ mvis cam-imu -c cam_imu.yaml

# apply the result and recalibrate; PASS / FAIL
$ mvis roundtrip -c cam_imu.yaml

      One self-contained HTML report per run.

      Not in Kalibr or Basalt

      Six things the common tools do not do.

      Kalibr and Basalt are the open-source camera–IMU toolboxes most people use. Neither covers these six cases. See the full comparison.

      1. Rolling shutter + IMU

        A rolling-shutter camera reads its rows one after another. MVIS solves that readout time together with the IMU. Kalibr's rolling-shutter tool is camera-only; Basalt has none.

        GuideWhy it matters

      2. Global + rolling shutter + IMU

        Global-shutter and rolling-shutter cameras on one rig, solved with all its IMUs in one run. Our VI-Rig recording has 2 global-shutter cameras, 2 rolling-shutter cameras and 4 IMUs.

        ExampleConfig

      3. Board shape and pose

        MVIS solves the board pose and, optionally, one height per corner (estimate.board_points). Kalibr and Basalt assume a flat board. On our VI-Rig mount, a flat solve biases the focal length by up to 1 % and the gyro scale by about 1 %.

        Why it matters

      4. Gyro-to-accel time offset

        t_ga is the time offset between an IMU's gyroscope and its accelerometer. MVIS fits it by default in camera–IMU runs. Kalibr and Basalt use one IMU clock, so the offset leaks into the camera time offset.

        Why it matters

      5. Digital-twin simulation

        A digital twin is a simulated copy of your recording where the truth is known. MVIS re-solves it and reports the error of each parameter, so you can test the solver on your own rig.

        Guide

      6. Round-trip verification

        mvis roundtrip applies the calibration to its own data and calibrates again. It reports PASS or FAIL without ground truth, and exits non-zero on FAIL so it can run in CI.

        Guide

      Benchmark

      Checked against Kalibr on TUM-VI.

      0.2px

      largest median focal-length (fx, fy) difference from Kalibr; principal point within 0.35 px. Camera-only stereo, 8 raw recordings, view angles cut to 85° or less for every tool

      <0.1mm

      median stereo-baseline difference, inside the 0.1 mm run-to-run spread; same 8 recordings

      1.8mm

      largest median lever-arm difference (camera position in the IMU frame), 4 raw and 4 512×512 recordings. Kalibr was given MVIS's intrinsics and baseline here

      0.9ms

      median camera time offset above Kalibr's; Basalt is within 0.1 ms of Kalibr. MVIS also fits t_ga (−1.5 to −1.6 ms here), which Kalibr does not have

      Benchmark batch 2026-11-10a. See the Leaderboard and each device's calibration.

      It checks its own work

      A low residual is not the test.

      A low reprojection error says a calibration fits its own data, not that it is right. In a round trip, PASS needs the IMU intrinsics and biases to collapse to identity and zero, and the geometry to hold. See Round-trip validation.
