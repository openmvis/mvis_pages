Demo and values

Section titled “Demo and values”

Depth camera from RealSense with a 50 mm global-shutter infrared stereo pair, a rolling-shutter RGB camera and a 6-axis IMU. Four hand-held recordings in front of an AprilGrid; the first, 95 s long, is shown here, solved as RGB + left infrared + right infrared + IMU with the RGB rolling-shutter readout estimated.

Reprojection median0.08 px
cam0–cam1 baseline14.6 mm
cam0 td, first row+6.26 ms+22.46 ms at mid-frame
cam0 lever arm30.4 mm

Reprojection is how far, in pixels, the detected corners lie from where the solve puts them. td is the camera–IMU time offset, and the lever arm is the camera–IMU position offset.

We ran the same solve on all four recordings. They agree on the RGB lever arm across the image (x within 0.3 mm), the time offset (within 0.08 ms), the readout time (32.33 to 32.42 ms), the focal length (within 1.2 px) and the infrared baseline (49.98 to 50.08 mm, against the 50.05 mm stored in the camera). They do not agree along the optical axis: the RGB lever z is 17.7 to 21.8 mm, a 4.1 mm spread, and every camera sits 6 to 10 mm above the RealSense SDK's design values. The infrared cameras spread as much as the RGB camera, so the rolling shutter is not the cause. All four recordings turn the camera little about the axes across the image, which is what makes that distance observable, so z is not settled.

Calibration process

Section titled “Calibration process”

The path of the rig around the board, as MVIS solved it. To explore it:

  • Press Play or drag the bar to follow the path.
  • Use the view buttons to change the view.
  • Pick a camera card to put that camera on the frustum, the pyramid that shows its view.

Each card draws the board from the solved pose, with the corners MVIS detected. We publish no camera images.

The time offsets, to scale. td is the camera–IMU time offset, with t_imu = t_cam + td. For a rolling-shutter camera td is the time of the first image row; the middle row is td + readout / 2, which is what a global-shutter calibration reports.

The calibration board isn't perfectly flat, so this solve estimated the height of every corner off the flat board along with the calibration. The board's mean plane and tilt belong to its pose, so a flat board shows only noise here.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • We ran the same solve on all four recordings. They agree on the RGB lever arm across the image (x within 0.3 mm), the time offset (within 0.08 ms), the readout time (32.33 to 32.42 ms), the focal length (within 1.2 px) and the infrared baseline (49.98 to 50.08 mm, against the 50.05 mm stored in the camera). They do not agree along the optical axis: the RGB lever z is 17.7 to 21.8 mm, a 4.1 mm spread, and every camera sits 6 to 10 mm above the RealSense SDK's design values. The infrared cameras spread as much as the RGB camera, so the rolling shutter is not the cause. All four recordings turn the camera little about the axes across the image, which is what makes that distance observable, so z is not settled.
  • The tag size, 88 mm, is the stated size of the printed board, and it scales every lever arm and baseline.
  • The board is not flat: its corners lie 6.4 to 7.1 mm peak to peak out of plane over the four recordings.
  • The camera stores no IMU correction, so the IMU scale and bias values are those of the raw sensor. IMU noise is the MVIS default, and the gyro's sensitivity to acceleration is not estimated.
  • The infrared distortion comes out close to zero, as expected if the camera delivers rectified infrared images, so those values describe that output, not the bare lens.

How it was run

Section titled “How it was run”
  • Each camera first solved on its own, then the RGB and both infrared cameras with the IMU in one solve. The RGB camera is a rolling-shutter camera with the readout time estimated; the infrared pair is global shutter. Pinhole cameras with radial-tangential distortion, seeded from the single-camera solves and left free; the camera time offsets, IMU scale and misalignment and the gyro-to-accel offset t_ga estimated. The board shape is estimated per corner. IMU noise is the MVIS default, since no measured values exist for this unit. Gravity is the local value at the recording site, 9.7892 m/s² (UAE). Solved 2026-11-24.
