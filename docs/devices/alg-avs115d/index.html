GMSL2 stereo camera module from Shenzhen Aili-Light Technology (ALG Tech, 艾利光), with two 1920×1920 rolling-shutter cameras 65 mm apart and a 6-axis IMU. Two hand-held recordings in front of a ChArUco board, the second in stronger light with a much shorter exposure, each solved as stereo + IMU with each camera's readout time estimated.

Reprojection is how far, in pixels, the detected corners lie from where the solve puts them. td is the camera–IMU time offset, and the lever arm is the camera–IMU position offset.

No independent reference on this page. These values show what MVIS solved, not how close they are to the truth. The two recordings agree within 1.4 mm on the lever arm, 0.15 % on the gyro scales, 0.8 ms on the readout and 0.1 mm on the baseline.

Calibration process

Section titled “Calibration process”

The path of the rig around the board, as MVIS solved it. To explore it:

  • Press Play or drag the bar to follow the path.
  • Use the view buttons to change the view.
  • Pick a camera card to put that camera on the frustum, the pyramid that shows its view.

Each card draws the board from the solved pose, with the corners MVIS detected. We publish no camera images.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • No independent reference on this page. These values show what MVIS solved, not how close they are to the truth. The two recordings agree within 1.4 mm on the lever arm, 0.15 % on the gyro scales, 0.8 ms on the readout and 0.1 mm on the baseline.
  • The camera-only solve, without the IMU, puts the readout at 25 ms (recording 1) and 21 ms (recording 2) against 31–32 ms here; the board's motion alone pins it less well, so the joint value is the one shown.
  • In a simulated copy of recording 1, with noise matched to this unit, MVIS recovered the lever arm within 1.2 mm, the rotation within 0.3 mrad, the time offset within 0.5 ms, the readout within 0.6 ms and the gyro scales within 0.7 %. With MVIS's default IMU noise the same test drifts (lever arm 6 mm along the optical axis, gyro scales 1 to 1.6 %), which is why this solve uses the tighter noise.
  • The camera–IMU time offset differs between the recordings: td is −14.3 ms in recording 1 and −1.4 ms in recording 2, each ± 0.1 ms, while everything else repeats. The likely cause is the exposure time. The driver stamps each image a fixed 45 ms before it reaches the host in both bags, so the stamp follows the readout. td refers to the middle of the first row's exposure, which comes before the readout by half the exposure time: a longer exposure moves it earlier and makes td more negative.
  • The bags don't record the exposure time. Measured from the motion blur, recording 1 was exposed for about 18 to 21 ms and recording 2 for about 2 to 5 ms. Half of that change is about 8 ms: the right direction, but short of the 13 ms measured, and the rest is still open. On the Dibuli Prism A4L, two exposures 1 ms apart moved td by 0.5 to 0.7 ms per ms of exposure, close to what this explanation predicts.
  • In practice, td on this module depends on the exposure, so with auto-exposure it is not one number. Stamp frames at mid-exposure if the driver allows it, or use the td measured at an exposure close to yours; recording 2's value suits short exposures.

How it was run

Section titled “How it was run”
  • Both cameras and the IMU, rolling shutter with each camera's readout time estimated, Kannala–Brandt (kb4) lenses with intrinsics free, IMU noise set about 5× tighter than MVIS's default to match this unit, t_ga estimated. Fast motion blurred some frames; stereo pairs whose board edges were more than 1.5× wider than the median were left out (17 % of the pairs in recording 1, 0.6 % in recording 2). The IMU stored accel 4× and gyro 2× too small; both were scaled before solving. Solved 2026-11-14 (recording 1) and 2026-11-15 (recording 2), with the same settings.
