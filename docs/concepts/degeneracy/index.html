Degeneracy

Use this page when a calibration value looks wrong or varies between recordings, and you suspect the motion.

A motion is degenerate for a value when it gives the data no way to measure that value (Observability explains the reasons). A degenerate value does not make the solve fail. It converges, the residuals look fine, and the value sits near its start or drifts with noise. So you have to look for it.

Symbols follow Notation & glossary.

How MVIS shows it

Section titled “How MVIS shows it”

MVIS does not stop a run because the motion was weak. It reports, at four points.

Before the solve

Section titled “Before the solve”
  • Recording length: mvis inspect warns when a recording with an IMU is shorter than 30 s.
  • Window choice: for bags longer than 80 s, the camera–IMU run keeps the 80 s with the most gyro and accelerometer excitation, scored per IMU as the mean squared angular rate plus the variance of the acceleration magnitude (input.window_mode: motion).

In the start values

Section titled “In the start values”

The start-value step runs simple fits before the joint solve and checks them:

  • Rotation excitation: the singular values of the camera–gyro angular-velocity fit. One large and two tiny ones mean rotation about one axis.
  • Time-offset sharpness: how much the fit error rises away from the best offset. A flat minimum means the offset is weak.
  • Lever arm: if its 1σ in the linear fit is over 5 cm, it starts at 0.
  • Accelerometer bias: it starts at 0. The start step estimates it only when asked (an advanced start option); it then falls back to 0 when the linear fit cannot separate it (smallest singular value of the scaled system under 10⁻³, or 1σ over 0.05 m/s²).
  • Gravity and scale: the fitted gravity should be within 5 % of the configured ggg (gravity: or site:, 9.81 by default) and the scale within 3 % of 1. A miss points at wrong intrinsics, units or stamps.
  • Auxiliary IMUs: the gyro-to-gyro start needs a clear minimum (1.5 times sharper than the edges) and more than 10 s of overlap.

A value that starts at 0 is still solved jointly; the start only keeps a poor guess out.

In the joint solve

Section titled “In the joint solve”

Freed IMU intrinsics carry a weak prior (0.1 on Dw\mathbf{D}_wDw​ and Da\mathbf{D}_aDa​). On a direction the motion does not excite, that prior wins: the value stays near its start instead of wandering. Its 1σ then stays close to the prior’s 0.1: that is the sign the motion did not excite it.

In the results

Section titled “In the results”
  • 1σ for every value: from the marginal covariance of the joint solve, in the result JSON and the report. Compare each with the others of its kind and across recordings.
  • Camera-only report, motion grades: each camera gets a grade (good, marginal, poor) per value, from its board poses. The thresholds are empirical:
Grade forMeasureGoodMarginal
focal lengthfarthest over nearest board distanceover 2.0over 1.5
distortionimage area covered by the board cornersover 60 %over 30 %
rotation diversitysmallest over largest spread of the rotation axesover 0.1over 0.03
time offsetspread of the angular speedover 0.5 rad/sover 0.2 rad/s
readoutpeak angular speed about the camera’s x and y axesover 1.0 rad/sover 0.3 rad/s
  • Camera–IMU report, motion and excitation: the angular velocity of the solved trajectory over time. Gaps and one-axis stretches are easy to see.
  • mvis verdict: grades a result green, yellow or red. It flags a χ² per degree of freedom (the weighted squared error divided by its degrees of freedom, about 1 when the noise model fits) over 1.5 (yellow) or 3.0 (red), a solve that hit its iteration cap, an IMU scale more than 5 % from 1, and a camera time offset over 50 ms. A wrong value from weak motion can still pass these; the 1σ and a second recording are the stronger checks.

Check it yourself

Section titled “Check it yourself”
  • Calibrate two or more recordings. A value that moves far more than its 1σ between them was not observed.
  • Run the digital twin. MVIS replays your motion on a simulated copy of your rig with known truth, solves it, and shows which values come back (step 7: digital-twin check).
  • Run the round trip. Correct the data with the result and calibrate again; if the first result was right, the second finds nothing left to correct (step 8: round-trip validation).
